Meta says your ads have a 2.4 ROAS. Is that good? It depends on one number most small store owners never calculate: your **break-even ROAS**. This guide shows you how to work it out in one minute, what to do with it, and how to set a daily budget that won't burn cash.

## What ROAS means

ROAS (return on ad spend) is revenue divided by ad spend. If you spend $100 and the ads bring $300 in sales, your ROAS is 3.0.

ROAS alone doesn't tell you if you made money. A 3.0 ROAS is a profit for a candle with a 70% margin and a loss for a phone case with a 25% margin.

## The break-even ROAS formula

```
Break-even ROAS = Price ÷ (Price − Cost per order)
```

"Cost per order" is everything you pay to deliver one sale except ads: product cost, packaging, shipping you cover, and payment or marketplace fees.

**Example.** You sell a tote bag for $30. Product, packaging, shipping and fees come to $18.

- Profit before ads: $30 − $18 = $12
- Break-even ROAS: $30 ÷ $12 = **2.5**

So every $1 of ads must bring at least $2.50 in sales just to break even. Below 2.5 you lose money on every order. Above 2.5 you profit.

## Quick reference table

| Margin before ads | Break-even ROAS |
|---|---|
| 70% | 1.43 |
| 50% | 2.0 |
| 40% | 2.5 |
| 33% | 3.0 |
| 25% | 4.0 |
| 20% | 5.0 |

If your break-even ROAS is above 5.0 (margin under 20%), fix price or bundle products before spending on ads. Ads rarely rescue a thin margin.

## Break-even CPA: the same idea per order

```
Break-even CPA = Price − Cost per order
```

In the tote bag example, you can pay up to **$12** in ads per sale. Meta shows this as "cost per purchase". If it is above $12, that ad set is losing money.

## Set a target, not just a floor

Breaking even pays nobody. A simple rule many store owners use:

- **Target ROAS** = break-even ROAS × 1.3 → 2.5 × 1.3 = **3.25**
- **Target CPA** = break-even CPA × 0.75 → $12 × 0.75 = **$9**

## How to pick a daily budget from these numbers

1. Decide the most you can spend in a month, for example $600.
2. Daily max = $600 ÷ 30 = **$20**.
3. Start at about 60% of that: **$12/day**.
4. Raise by 20% only when the last 3 days beat your target ROAS and the last 7 days beat break-even.
5. Don't touch budget, audience or creative for the first 3 days while Meta learns.

## When Meta's ROAS is wrong

If you also sell on Etsy, Amazon or in person, Meta can't see those sales. Your real ROAS may be higher than Ads Manager shows. Add up all sales in the ad period, compare to your normal week, and use the extra revenue for a fair estimate.
